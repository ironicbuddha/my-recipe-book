Dry-brined roast chicken served with a concentrated cider jus.

## PHASE A — DRY-BRINE THE CHICKEN

**Timing:** Start 12–24 h before service; 10 min active.

### Ingredient Uses

| Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use                                   |
| ---------- | -------: | ------: | ------------------------------------- |
| Chicken    |   1600 g | 100.00% | Whole, patted dry; primary subject     |
| Fine salt  |     16 g |   1.00% | Applied evenly under and over the skin |

### Technique Applications

| Technique   | Controls                         | Purpose                                  |
| ----------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Dry brining | 1% salt; uncovered refrigeration | Season throughout and dehydrate the skin |

### Method

1. Salt the chicken evenly under and over the skin.
2. Refrigerate uncovered on a rack for 12–24 h.

### Produces

| Phase Output       | Description                              |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Dry-brined chicken | Seasoned chicken with a dry skin surface |

### Principles

- **Osmotic seasoning** — time allows dissolved salt to diffuse into the meat.
- **Surface dehydration** — a drier skin browns and crisps more readily.

## PHASE B — PREPARE THE CIDER BASE

**Timing:** 20 min active.

**May overlap:** PHASE A.

### Ingredient Uses

| Ingredient    | Quantity | Scaling | Use                                      |
| ------------- | -------: | ------: | ---------------------------------------- |
| Dry cider     |    250 g |  15.63% | Reduced to concentrate acidity and fruit |
| Chicken stock |    250 g |  15.63% | Added after the cider reduction          |

### Technique Applications

| Technique | Controls                           | Purpose                               |
| --------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Reduction | Gentle boil; reduce to 200 g total | Concentrate flavour without scorching |

### Method

1. Reduce the cider by half over medium heat.
2. Add the stock and reduce the combined liquid to 200 g.
3. Cool and reserve.

### Produces

| Phase Output | Description                            |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------- |
| Cider base   | Concentrated cider and stock reduction |

### Principles

- **Volatile concentration** — controlled evaporation intensifies flavour while retaining usable acidity.

## PHASE C — ROAST AND FINISH

**Timing:** Begin 75 min before service; rest 20 min.

### Consumes

| Phase Output       | Use                                                |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Dry-brined chicken | All; roasted until the breast reaches its endpoint |
| Cider base         | 200 g; deglazes the roasting pan and forms the jus |

### Ingredient Uses

| Ingredient      |  Quantity | Scaling | Use                           |
| --------------- | --------: | ------: | ----------------------------- |
| Unsalted butter |      30 g |   1.88% | Whisked into the jus off heat |
| Fine salt       | As needed |       — | Final jus adjustment only     |

### Technique Applications

| Technique          | Controls                            | Purpose                                       |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| High-heat roasting | 220 C oven; breast endpoint 64 C    | Brown the skin while limiting breast overcook |
| Resting            | 20 min, uncovered                   | Equalise temperature and retain juices        |
| Emulsification     | Whisk cold butter in below a simmer | Give the jus body and gloss                   |

### Method

1. Roast the dry-brined chicken at 220 C until the breast reaches 64 C.
2. Rest the chicken uncovered for 20 min.
3. Deglaze the roasting pan with the cider base and reduce to sauce consistency.
4. Remove from heat, whisk in the butter, and adjust with salt.
5. Carve the chicken and serve with the cider jus.

### Principles

- **Carryover cooking** — the breast continues heating during the rest.
- **Emulsion stability** — lower heat helps butter remain dispersed in the aqueous jus.

## FAILURE MODES

| Symptom   | Likely cause                                      | Corrective action                            |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Pale skin | Chicken surface remained wet before PHASE C       | Extend uncovered refrigeration after PHASE A |
| Split jus | Butter was incorporated while the jus was too hot | Cool slightly and whisk in cold water        |
