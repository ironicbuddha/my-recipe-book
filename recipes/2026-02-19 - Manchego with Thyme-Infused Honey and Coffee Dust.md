Sweet–bitter contrast plate. Best served at 18–20 C (cool room temperature).

## PHASE A — CHEESE PREPARATION

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| manchego | [Aged Manchego (12+ months)](ref:ingredient/manchego) | 100 g | 100.00% | Aged Manchego (12+ months). |

### Method

1. Remove Manchego from refrigeration 45–60 min before serving.
2. Slice into 5–7 mm wedges or thick shards. Arrange on a neutral plate.

### Phase Outputs

| Key | Phase Output | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| prepared-manchego | Prepared Manchego | Tempered, sliced cheese. |

Aged Manchego (12+ months) is essential to the source profile: young cheese lacks the crystalline texture and concentrated flavour to stand up to the bitterness.

## PHASE B — THYME HONEY

**May overlap:** PHASE A — CHEESE PREPARATION while the cheese tempers.

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| honey | [Good-quality honey (wildflower preferred)](ref:ingredient/honey) | 30 g | 30.00% | Good-quality honey (wildflower preferred). |
| thyme | [Fresh thyme leaves](ref:ingredient/thyme) | 1 g | 1.00% | Fresh thyme leaves. |

### Technique Applications

| Technique | Controls | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Infusion](ref:technique/infusion) | 40–45 C; 10–15 min | Transfer thyme aroma into warm honey. |

### Principles

- [Aromatic infusion](ref:principle/aromatic-infusion) — Gentle honey warming carries thyme aroma without the source cooked-herb bitterness.

### Method

1. Warm honey gently to 40–45 C (just fluid, not hot).
2. Stir in thyme leaves and allow to infuse 10–15 min.
3. Strain or leave leaves in for a rustic finish.
4. Aim for aromatic lift without cooked-herb bitterness; keep honey at 40–45 C and avoid exceeding 50 C.

### Phase Outputs

| Key | Phase Output | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thyme-honey | Thyme honey | Warm thyme-infused honey, strained or left rustic. |

## PHASE C — COFFEE DUST

**May overlap:** PHASE B — THYME HONEY during infusion.

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| coffee-beans | [Whole coffee beans (medium roast)](ref:ingredient/coffee-beans) | 2.5 g | 2.50% | Whole coffee beans (medium roast). |

### Technique Applications

| Technique | Controls | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Fine grinding](ref:technique/fine-grinding) | Very fine coffee powder | Prepare a restrained seasoning. |
| [Sifting](ref:technique/sifting) | Fine sieve; remove coarse particles | Avoid concentrated bitter hits. |

### Method

1. Grind coffee beans very fine, almost powder.
2. Sift through a fine sieve to remove coarse particles.
3. Use sparingly — think seasoning, not crust.
4. Optionally bloom the coffee powder in a dry pan for 20 s, as the source refinement suggests, to soften acidity.

### Phase Outputs

| Key | Phase Output | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| coffee-dust | Coffee dust | Fine sifted coffee powder. |

Medium roast works better than dark in this plate. Coffee should register as roast complexity rather than a dominant coffee flavour; the source records no measured flavour threshold.

## PHASE D — FINISH AND ASSEMBLY

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| finishing-salt | [Sea salt](ref:ingredient/salt) | As needed | — | Optional tiny flake to sharpen contrast. |

### Principles

- [Sweet bitter contrast](ref:principle/sweet-bitter-contrast) — Honey sweetness balances a restrained coffee dusting.
- [Flavour threshold management](ref:principle/flavour-threshold-management) — Keep coffee as roast complexity rather than the dominant flavour.

### Method

1. Drizzle warm thyme honey lightly over or beside the cheese.
2. Dust with a restrained pinch of coffee powder.
3. Serve immediately.

### Phase Outputs Used

| Phase Output | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared Manchego | Use all of `prepared-manchego` from PHASE A as directed above. |
| Thyme honey | Drizzle `thyme-honey` lightly over or beside the cheese. |
| Coffee dust | Use only a restrained pinch of `coffee-dust`. |

## FAILURE MODES

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Coffee flavour dominates the plate. | Excessive coffee dust or grind too coarse (larger particles deliver more concentrated hits). | Reduce to a bare pinch; sift more aggressively to remove coarse particles. |
| Honey tastes flat or cloying. | Honey not warm enough to volatilise thyme aromatics, or infusion time too short. | Warm to 40–45 C and infuse a full 15 min. Use wildflower or thyme-origin honey for better baseline complexity. |
| Cheese tastes rubbery and muted. | Served too cold — fat crystallisation suppresses flavour release and texture. | Ensure full 45–60 min tempering at room temperature before serving. |

## OPTIONAL REFINEMENTS

- Add a tiny flake of [sea salt](ref:ingredient/salt) to sharpen contrast.
- The optional 20 s coffee-powder pan refinement is preserved in PHASE C.
- Serve a dry [fino sherry](ref:ingredient/fino-sherry) alongside if desired.
