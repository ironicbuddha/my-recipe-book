French-style custard ice cream with pronounced dark maple flavour, restrained sweetness, and a dense, smooth, glossy texture. Optional reduced maple ripple adds soft maple seams.

## PHASE A — HEAT DAIRY BASE

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| whole-milk | [Whole milk](ref:ingredient/whole-milk) | 300 g | 100.00% | Form the dairy base. |
| cream | [Cream, approximately 35% fat](ref:ingredient/heavy-cream) | 220 g | 73.33% | Supply fat and richness. |
| caster-sugar | [Caster sugar](ref:ingredient/caster-sugar) | 20 g | 6.67% | Use the supplied alternative to maple sugar. |
| glucose-syrup | [Glucose syrup](ref:ingredient/glucose-syrup) | 20 g | 6.67% | Contribute to the sugar system and texture. |
| skim-milk-powder | [Skim milk powder](ref:ingredient/skim-milk-powder) | 25 g | 8.33% | Supply body and water-binding without increasing fat. |
| base-salt | [Fine sea salt](ref:ingredient/salt) | 1.5 g | 0.50% | Intensify the maple flavour. |

### Method

1. Combine the milk, cream, glucose syrup, skim milk powder, salt, and caster sugar in a saucepan.
2. Whisk thoroughly to disperse the milk powder.
3. Heat gently to 70 C–75 C, stirring occasionally.

This formulation uses the selected 20 g caster sugar alternative. The original proposal uses 25 g maple sugar (8.33% of the milk basis) for stronger maple character without additional syrup water.

| Key | Phase Output | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hot-dairy | Hot dairy mixture | All of the heated mixture for tempering and cooking the yolks. |

## PHASE B — TEMPER YOLKS AND COOK CUSTARD

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| egg-yolks | [Egg yolks](ref:ingredient/egg-yolk) | 70 g | 23.33% | Emulsify the base and add custard richness. |

### Method

1. Place the egg yolks in a separate bowl and whisk until smooth.
2. Slowly pour approximately one-third of the hot dairy mixture into the yolks while whisking continuously.
3. Return the tempered yolk mixture to the saucepan containing the remaining dairy mixture.
4. Cook over low to medium-low heat, stirring continuously with a silicone spatula, until the custard reaches 82 C–84 C and lightly coats the spatula.
5. Remove immediately from the heat. Do not allow the custard to boil.

## PHASE C — INCORPORATE MAPLE AND MATURE

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| base-maple-syrup | [Pure maple syrup, preferably dark/robust](ref:ingredient/maple-syrup) | 110 g | 36.67% | Add the principal maple flavour after cooking. |
| vanilla | [Vanilla extract or paste](ref:ingredient/vanilla-extract) | 2 g | 0.67% | Supply a restrained background accent. |

### Method

1. Add the maple syrup and vanilla to the hot cooked custard.
2. Blend with an immersion blender or high-speed blender for approximately 30 s–60 s, until completely smooth and emulsified.
3. Transfer to a clean container and chill rapidly over an ice bath until below 10 C.
4. Cover and refrigerate for 8 h–24 h.

Use 100% pure maple syrup rather than maple-flavoured pancake syrup. Dark/robust syrup is preferred because delicate amber syrup can lose its flavour in the dairy base. Adding the principal syrup after cooking is intended to preserve more of its volatile aroma.

Maple syrup supplies both sugars and substantial water; increasing it indefinitely can soften the ice cream. Maple sugar adds flavour without that extra water. The supplied formulation deliberately uses less glucose than its peanut butter precursor because maple syrup also contributes sugars that depress the freezing point. Maturation hydrates the milk solids and allows the fat, protein, and water phases to equilibrate. Keep vanilla in the background and salt below a perceptibly salty level.

## PHASE D — PREPARE OPTIONAL MAPLE RIPPLE

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ripple-maple-syrup | [Pure maple syrup](ref:ingredient/maple-syrup) | As needed | — | Use 20 g–30 g for the optional ripple (6.67%–10.00% of the milk basis). |
| ripple-salt | [Fine sea salt](ref:ingredient/salt) | As needed | — | Add a small pinch to the optional ripple. |

### Method

1. Skip this Phase if making the plain maple version.
2. Combine the ripple maple syrup with a small pinch of salt.
3. Simmer gently until reduced by approximately 20%–25%. Do not reduce aggressively into caramel.
4. Cool completely. Keep the syrup slightly thicker than ordinary maple syrup but still fluid when refrigerated.

## PHASE E — FREEZE BASE

### Method

1. Transfer the chilled matured maple base into the freezing container.
2. Freeze completely according to the frozen-dessert system being used. For the Cuisinart Freeze Wand, freeze the container level for at least 18 h–24 h.

## PHASE F — SPIN AND FINISH

### Ingredient Uses

| Key | Ingredient | Quantity | Scaling | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| respin-milk | [Whole milk](ref:ingredient/whole-milk) | As needed | — | Add approximately 10 g–15 g only if powdery or crumbly (3.33%–5.00% of the milk basis). |

### Method

1. Process the frozen maple base using the normal Ice Cream programme.
2. Inspect the texture. If powdery or crumbly, add approximately 10 g–15 g whole milk.
3. Respin until dense, smooth, and glossy.
4. If the Mix-In programme continues to provide the smooth finishing texture described for the supplied peanut butter recipe, use it as an additional finishing spin.

Recorded variations from the supplied proposal; no separate trial observations were supplied:

- **Maple pecan:** Toast 70 g–90 g [pecans](ref:ingredient/pecans) at 160 C for 8 min–10 min, cool completely, chop coarsely, and incorporate using the Mix-In programme. An additional proposed variation tosses the pecans with a little brown butter, maple syrup, and bourbon before cooling and folding through; quantities were not supplied.
- **Maple bourbon:** Add 10 g–15 g [bourbon](ref:ingredient/bourbon) after cooking the custard in PHASE C. Keep the amount modest because alcohol lowers the freezing point.
- **Maple bacon:** Fold through finely chopped, exceptionally crisp [bacon](ref:ingredient/bacon) with a small amount of maple ripple. A bacon quantity was not supplied.
- **Maple and dark chocolate:** Add 60 g–70 g thin [dark-chocolate](ref:ingredient/dark-chocolate) shards. The proposal favours 70% chocolate for bitterness against the maple; shard preparation details were not supplied.

## PHASE G — ASSEMBLE, HARDEN, AND SERVE

### Method

1. For the plain version, transfer the spun maple ice cream directly into a storage container. For the ripple version, transfer approximately one-third first and drizzle over a small amount of cold maple ripple.
2. For the ripple version, add another layer of ice cream, repeat with additional syrup, and finish with the remaining ice cream.
3. Make one or two shallow folds with a knife or narrow spatula. Do not completely incorporate the syrup.
4. Cover tightly and freeze for approximately 2 h–4 h before serving.
5. Temper for approximately 5 min–10 min at room temperature before scooping.

Target an unmistakably maple first impression, followed by cream and subtle custard richness, with vanilla and sea salt as background accents. The intended flavour evokes maple candy, warm waffles, and browned dairy while keeping maple central; the supplied base uses whole milk and cream and does not specify cultured dairy.

## FAILURE MODES

| Symptom | Likely cause | Corrective action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Powdery or crumbly after processing | The frozen base needs additional processing or a little liquid. | Add approximately 10 g–15 g whole milk and respin until smooth. |
| Weak maple flavour | Delicate syrup is diluted by the dairy; the selected caster sugar alternative adds less maple flavour. | Use dark/robust pure syrup and consider the optional ripple. |
| Vanilla dominates | Excess vanilla overwhelms the maple. | Keep vanilla at the supplied 2 g dose. |
| Ice cream sets too softly | Extra syrup or bourbon increases freezing-point depression. | Preserve the supplied base quantities and keep bourbon within the proposed range. |
| Custard boils or becomes grainy | Heat is too high or cooking continues beyond the control range. | Stir continuously over low to medium-low heat and remove at 82 C–84 C; do not boil. |
| Ripple tastes caramelised or becomes too thick | Syrup was reduced too aggressively. | Limit reduction to approximately 20%–25% and keep the cooled syrup fluid. |
| Maple seams disappear | Ripple was folded in too thoroughly. | Layer the cold syrup and make only one or two shallow folds. |
